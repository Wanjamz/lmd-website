Will Athi River Flood Again?

What the April 2024 disaster tells us about the very strong 2026 El Niño, and whether Mavoko is ready for the October to December short rains.

Abstract

On 24 April 2024 the Athi River broke its banks before dawn and flooded Kwa Mang'eli, Graceland Estate, Kicheko, Kasuitu and neighbouring settlements in Mavoko, Machakos County. Around 2,000 people were displaced, two died, and rescue teams used boats to reach families on rooftops. Two and a half years later, a very strong El Niño is building in the Pacific and Kenya's forecasters expect an unusually wet October to December 2026 season.

This paper asks whether Athi River could flood again this season, and whether anything has changed to prevent it. It is a desk review of 30+ sources, including NOAA, ICPAC, the Kenya Meteorological Service Authority (KMSA), OCHA and NDOC situation reports, a World Weather Attribution study, two peer-reviewed catchment studies, the Mavoko Municipality Integrated Development Plan 2024 to 2029, court records reported in the press, and news coverage from 2018 to September 2026. Elevations were checked against the SRTM 30 m digital elevation model and site locations against OpenStreetMap. Every claim carries an evidence rating.

Our answer: a damaging flood in Athi River during the 2026 short rains is plausible and the risk is elevated, but it is not certain. The rainfall hazard has risen sharply. The drivers that turned heavy rain into a disaster in 2024 (a fast-draining upstream catchment, rapid paving of the municipality, homes on low ground beside the river, and undersized or blocked drains) were still producing floods in Mavoko as recently as April and May 2026. Authorities have announced riparian demolitions, drain clearance and evacuation planning, but this review found no public evidence that any structural flood protection for the main Athi River corridor has been completed.

Keywords: Athi River; Mavoko; El Niño 2026; short rains; riverine flooding; urban flooding; riparian encroachment; Machakos County; early warning.

Read this first: what this paper is and is not

This is desk research. The author has not visited the affected sites during this review and cannot confirm on-the-ground conditions, the state of individual drains, or whether specific works have been built. Section 01

01Key findings

1

The 2026 hazard is real and near. NOAA's September update gives a greater than 90% chance of a very strong El Niño and a 75% chance that October to December 2026 exceeds every El Niño on record back to 1950. ICPAC and KMSA both forecast above-normal short rains, with Nairobi, Machakos and Kajiado named. ★★★★★

2

The flood comes from upstream. The river that flooded Athi River drains the Ngong Hills and Kajiado North, falling roughly 470 m to the town. Heavy rain there can raise the river in Mavoko hours later. ★★★★

3

2024 was not simply an El Niño flood. World Weather Attribution found El Niño and the Indian Ocean Dipole had negligible influence on the March to May 2024 extreme rainfall, while climate change made it about twice as likely. El Niño's strongest link in Kenya is to the October to December season, which is exactly the season now approaching. ★★★★

4

The town is shedding more water than it used to. Mavoko's built-up area rose from 24.6% to 37.0% between 1989 and 2018, and the share of rain turned into runoff rose from 69% to 76%. Upstream, wetlands in the Mbagathi and Stony Athi sub-catchments fell by 99% between 2000 and 2019. ★★★

5

The vulnerabilities were still active in 2026. Floods hit Githunguri, Syokimau, Mlolongo, 360 Estate and Kicheko during the March to May 2026 rains, a season without El Niño influence. Officials blamed encroachment on riparian land, blocked waterways and diverted rivers. ★★★

6

Plans exist; delivery is unverified. Mavoko's 2024 to 2029 plan budgets KSh 100 million for stormwater drainage in Athi River and Mlolongo and proposes a disaster management centre. We found no public progress report. The plan treats flooding mainly as a drainage problem and does not set out river-corridor flood protection for the settlements hit in 2024. ★★★★

7

Most likely outcome: repeated local flooding, with a serious chance of a 2024-scale river flood. This is an analyst judgement, not a model output. The decisive unknowns are how much rain falls on the upper catchment in a single night, and whether warnings reach riverside households in time. Judgement

Section 02

02The morning the river rose

Heavy rain fell over Nairobi and its surroundings on the night of Tuesday 23 April 2024. At about 6 a.m. on Wednesday, the community policing chairman in Kwa Mang'eli received word that fast-moving water was heading for the village and blew a whistle to warn his neighbours; the village was soon submerged. [KNA, 25 Apr 2024 ★★★★] In Graceland Estate, a gated development where homes sold for about KSh 12 million, residents said water entered their houses at around 7 a.m. and rose quickly, in places reaching two floors. [KNA; Daily Nation, 24 Apr 2024 ★★★]

The Kenya Red Cross, working with a multi-agency team and residents, rescued 96 people from Graceland. [KRCS via The Star, 25 Apr 2024 ★★★] Police reported that about 1,500 people held by floodwater in Kwa Mang'eli were rescued around midday, and that two people died in the Athi River flooding. [The Star, 25 Apr 2024 ★★] The Machakos County Commissioner told KNA that more than 2,000 residents of Athi River sub-county were displaced and moved to rescue centres, naming Kwa Mang'eli, Kwa Nzomo, Kinanie, Kyelenzi, 39, Kisumu Ndogo and Graceland as the hardest hit. [KNA ★★★★] Governor Wavinya Ndeti listed Kicheko, Graceland, Kasuitu and Kwa Mang'eli as the worst affected and promised to evict developers on riparian land. [KNA; The Star ★★★]

Mavoko was one scene in a national emergency. By mid-June 2024, NDOC counted 315 deaths, 188 injuries, 38 people missing and more than 293,200 people displaced between 1 March and 18 June. [OCHA Flash Update 7 ★★★★] The Kenya Red Cross operations report later recorded 294 fatalities for March to May, about 55,000 displaced households, 11,000 livestock lost and 65,000 acres of cropland damaged. [Dialogue Earth, citing KRCS ★★★] Nairobi County was the worst affected, with an estimated 256,890 people affected by mid-May. [OCHA ★★★★] Five days after the Athi River flood, a debris-blocked culvert under a railway embankment failed at Mai Mahiu and killed dozens, a reminder that blocked drainage structures can turn water into a wave.

Section 03

03The 2026 El Niño: what forecasters now say

El Niño is no longer a forecast. NOAA's Climate Prediction Center has had an El Niño Advisory in place since June 2026. Its September discussion says the event is strengthening, gives a greater than 90% chance of a very strong event through the northern hemisphere autumn and winter, and puts a 75% chance on October to December 2026 exceeding the strength of every El Niño since 1950. It also cautions that a stronger event raises the odds of typical impacts without guaranteeing them. [NOAA CPC, Sept 2026 ★★★★★] The weekly Niño 3.4 index reached +2.7 °C in mid-August. [IRI ★★★★]

In simple terms

El Niño is a warming of the central and eastern Pacific that reshapes rainfall worldwide. East Africa is one of the few regions that tends to get wetter, mostly in the October to December "short rains". "Super" and "Godzilla" are media labels. NOAA's term is a very strong El Niño. A positive Indian Ocean Dipole, a warm western Indian Ocean, usually adds to East African rainfall and is also expected this year.

Regional and national outlooks

The 74th Greater Horn of Africa Climate Outlook Forum (GHACOF74), held on 17 and 18 August 2026, released an outlook indicating an increased likelihood of wetter-than-normal October to December conditions. ICPAC noted that this season supplies up to 70% of annual rainfall in parts of Kenya, named 1997 and 2023 as the closest analogue years, and expects a positive Indian Ocean Dipole. [ICPAC, 18 Aug 2026 ★★★★] Its technical statement gives a greater than 90% likelihood of exceeding 400 mm over parts of central Kenya during the season. [GHACOF74 ★★★★]

The Kenya Meteorological Service Authority's outlook, dated 26 August 2026, expects above-average rainfall over about 80% of the country, with prolonged wet spells and occasional isolated storms. Nairobi, Machakos and Kajiado are among the counties named. [KMSA via national press ★★★★] KMSA notes the short rains supply over 40% of annual rainfall in Machakos and parts of Kajiado. Onset is expected in the second to third week of October around Nairobi and in the third to fourth week in Machakos. [KMSA via The Kenya Times; Smart Farmer ★★★] This timing matters for Athi River, because the upper catchment may start running high before rain is heavy in Mavoko itself.

The economic stakes

In July, the African Development Bank's director for climate change and green growth told Reuters that the event could cut the GDP of heavily affected African countries by 1% to 2% on average, a combined $10 billion to $20 billion, and could drive migration from hard-hit areas. [Reuters, 26 Jul 2026 ★★★] This is a continental estimate, not a forecast for Athi River. For Kenya, the 1997 to 1998 El Niño floods are commonly estimated to have cost about 11% of annual GDP. [The Star infographic, Sept 2026 ★★]

Section 04

04Was 2024 an El Niño flood?

Popular coverage often called the 2024 floods "El Niño rains". The evidence is more precise than that, and the distinction changes what we should expect in 2026.

The World Meteorological Organization's State of the Climate in Africa 2024 said the El Niño and positive Indian Ocean Dipole that ran from 2023 into early 2024 played major roles in the continent's rainfall patterns. [WMO, May 2025 ★★★★] An event-specific study by World Weather Attribution, looking at the maximum 30-day rainfall of the 2024 long rains over southern Kenya, most of Tanzania and part of Burundi, reached a sharper conclusion: both El Niño and the Indian Ocean Dipole had negligible influence on that event. Human-caused warming made rainfall of that severity about twice as likely and about 5% more intense, with large statistical uncertainty. The authors also pointed to rapid urbanisation and settlement in flood-prone areas as amplifiers of the damage. [WWA, 23 May 2024 ★★★★]

The two findings are not contradictory. They cover different areas, periods and definitions. Together they give three lessons for Athi River:

Why this matters for 2026
  • A damaging flood did not need El Niño. The April 2024 flood happened in the long rains, when El Niño's influence on East Africa is weak.
  • 2026 is the season El Niño does affect. The October to December short rains carry East Africa's strongest El Niño signal, and the ICPAC analogue years, 1997 and 2023, both produced wet short rains across much of the region.
  • The background is shifting. A warmer atmosphere holds more moisture, so the same weather pattern can now deliver heavier bursts of rain than it did a generation ago.
Section 05

05Why Athi River floods: the causal chain

Athi River's floods come from two processes that often happen together and need different fixes.

ProcessWhat happensWhat reduces itWhat does not
Riverine floodingThe Athi River or a tributary carries more water than its channel can hold and spreads over its floodplain.Keeping the floodplain clear, upstream retention, channel and bridge capacity, early warning and evacuation.Clearing roadside drains alone.
Urban (pluvial) floodingIntense local rain cannot drain away because surfaces are paved and drains are missing, undersized or blocked.Adequate, maintained drains; open waterways; rainwater harvesting; permeable surfaces.River works alone.

Link 1: A steep, fast catchment upstream

The Mbagathi River, a main tributary of the upper Athi, rises in the Ngong Hills at about 1,980 m and flows east past Ongata Rongai and Nairobi National Park towards Athi River town. [Nyika 2017, via ResearchGate ★★★] Streams including the Kandisi join it near Rongai. Our check of the SRTM 30 m elevation model puts Athi River town centre at about 1,509 m and Graceland Estate at about 1,490 m, one of the lowest points among the sites reviewed. [SRTM via OpenTopoData ★★★★] The fall of roughly 470 m from the headwaters means rain over Ngong and Kajiado North reaches Mavoko quickly and with force.

Link 2: Paving the catchment

A 2020 remote-sensing study of Mavoko Municipality (820 km²) found that largely impervious built-up land rose from 24.6% of the area in 1989 to 37.0% in 2018, while grassland, open space and bare land fell from 65.5% to 44.5%. The share of annual rainfall converted into runoff rose from 69% to 76%, and 68% of the municipality produced more runoff than before. About 60% of Mavoko's soils are clays, which absorb water slowly. [IJERT, Apr 2020 ★★★] Upstream, a study of the Mbagathi and Stony Athi sub-catchments recorded a 53.67% increase in built-up area and a 99.32% loss of wetlands between 2000 and 2019, and projects seasonal rainfall rising by 12% to 21% by 2063 depending on the emissions pathway. [Sang & Olago ★★★]

Link 3: A shallower, narrower channel

When soil washes off degraded land upstream, it settles in the riverbed and reduces the volume the channel can carry. A NEMA official, speaking after a 2020 flood at Green Park Estate, described the Athi plains as low-lying, some areas below river flow level, and said silt and debris helped push the river more than 100 m beyond its bank. [Business Daily, Dec 2020 ★★] In court, a hydrologist attributed an earlier flood at the same estate to backwater from an obstruction during construction of a bridge across the Stony Athi on Mombasa Road. [The Standard ★★] We found no published sediment survey of the Athi channel through Mavoko, so the scale of siltation remains an open question.

Link 4: Building where the water goes

Mavoko's population was 322,499 in 2019 and is projected to reach about 593,000 by 2030, one of the fastest growth rates in the Nairobi Metropolitan Region. [IJERT, citing KNBS ★★★] Growth has pushed homes, walls and businesses onto river margins. In May 2026, after fresh floods, Machakos officials said sections of rivers and riparian areas in Syokimau, Mlolongo and Sabaki had been blocked by houses and perimeter walls, and that some rivers had been diverted. A residents' association chairman confirmed that some members had encroached and diverted a river. [The Star, 23 May 2026 ★★★]

Link 5: Drains that cannot cope

The Mavoko Integrated Development Plan 2024 to 2029 states that many municipal roads lack stormwater drains, that drains in central areas are often clogged with solid waste, and that this causes urban flooding. It documents flooding in Mlolongo Central and in Athi River's central area. [Mavoko IDeP, May 2024 ★★★★]

Correction to earlier drafts and graphics

Earlier working graphics linked Greatwall Gardens' legal troubles to flooding. That link is not supported. KCB's 2024 attempt to auction about 100 units stemmed from a loan dispute with the developer dating to 2015, not from flood damage. [The Standard; Business Daily; The Star, Apr to May 2024 ★★★] Similarly, the Green Park Court of Appeal ruling (August 2024) came after the April 2024 flood; the documented wall collapse at Green Park dates from 2018. These corrections do not reduce the risk; they keep the record accurate.

The difference in consequences is stark. For a household in a gated estate, a flood means ruined furniture, written-off cars and a stay with relatives. For families in informal riverside settlements, the same flood can mean the loss of the house itself, stock, livestock and a farm plot that fed the children. One Kwa Mang'eli resident told KNA she had lost her house and farm and had nothing left for her children. [KNA ★★★] Any flood plan that treats these groups the same will fail the poorest first.

What the plan leaves out

The IDeP was prepared between April and June 2024, in the weeks around the flood. It clearly recognises urban drainage failure. But a search of the document found no reference to the 24 April riverine flood, to Graceland, Kwa Mang'eli or Kicheko, or to river-corridor flood protection for them. Its disaster table focuses on fires, road accidents, collapsing buildings and disease, and addresses flash floods mainly through tree planting. The type of flood that did the most damage in 2024 is the type the plan says least about. [Mavoko IDeP ★★★★]

The March to May 2026 season is the most useful real-world test available. Without El Niño influence, heavy rain again flooded Mavoko. On 29 April 2026 more than 100 households were affected in Githunguri, and flooding hit Utawala, Katani, Airways, Kamulu and Syokimau. [The Star, 30 Apr 2026 ★★★] The municipal manager named 360 Estate, Kicheko and Kincar among the areas frequently hit. [Kenyans.co.ke, 3 May 2026 ★★] On 1 May, Mombasa Road was impassable and police warned that Nairobi's waterways, all draining into the Athi system, were running critically high. [Kenyans.co.ke ★★] Whatever has been done since 2024, it did not stop these floods.

Section 08

08Three scenarios for October to December 2026

These scenarios are analyst judgements built from the evidence above. They are not hydrological model outputs and carry no numerical probability.

A. Wet but managed

Lower likelihood

Trigger: rain is above normal but spread over many days, with no single extreme night over the upper catchment.

What it looks like: ponding on roads, traffic disruption on Mombasa and Namanga roads, the river high but mostly in channel.

Why less likely: KMSA expects prolonged wet spells, which saturate soils and reduce the margin before the next storm.

B. Repeated local floods

Most likely

Trigger: several intense storms over Mavoko and nearby suburbs.

What it looks like: storm water in homes in Syokimau, Mlolongo, Githunguri, 360 Estate and Kicheko; blocked culverts; road closures; small-scale displacement several times in the season.

Why most likely: this pattern occurred in April and May 2026 without El Niño, and the drivers are unchanged.

C. River and urban flood together

Serious possibility

Trigger: an extreme overnight storm over Ngong and Kajiado North on already saturated ground, as on 23 to 24 April 2024.

What it looks like: the Athi leaves its banks at Graceland, Kwa Mang'eli, Kicheko and Kasuitu before dawn; boat rescues; thousands displaced; possible deaths.

Why plausible: the forecast rainfall totals, analogue years and unaddressed floodplain exposure all point this way.

What would move us between scenarios
  • Towards C: an early and heavy onset in the Nairobi and Ngong highlands in mid-October; KMSA heavy-rainfall advisories above 50 mm in 24 hours for Nairobi and Kajiado; reports of the Mbagathi or Athi running high before Mavoko's own onset.
  • Towards A: published evidence that the 2024 hotspots have been cleared, drains built and a working warning chain set up; a weaker or delayed onset.

Section 10

10Recommendations

Each recommendation names who should act and by when. They are addressed to the season starting in October 2026.

  1. Machakos County Government and Municipal Board of Mavoko, before 10 October: publish a one-page status of drainage works, riparian clearances and rescue centres for Athi River town, Kinanie and Mlolongo, including Graceland, Kwa Mang'eli, Kicheko and Kasuitu. Evidence: IDeP targets; 2026 flood record.
  2. Machakos County Disaster Management Unit with the Kenya Red Cross, before onset: run door-to-door preparedness in the 2024 hotspots, confirm evacuation routes and pre-position boats at Athi River town, as the 2024 rescues depended on boats. Evidence: 2024 rescue accounts.
  3. Water Resources Authority (Athi Basin) and KMSA, before onset: set up an upstream-to-downstream alert so that heavy rain or high water on the Mbagathi triggers a warning to Mavoko ward administrators, chiefs and community policing committees. The only documented warning in 2024 was a whistle. Evidence: KNA eyewitness account.
  4. Kenya Urban Roads Authority, KeNHA and the Municipality, September to October: clear culverts and bridge openings on Mombasa Road, Namanga Road and estate roads that cross waterways. Evidence: IDeP; Mai Mahiu culvert failure; Green Park bridge testimony.
  5. Residents' associations in riverside estates, now: agree a household warning chain, move vehicles and valuables to higher ground when KMSA issues heavy-rain advisories, and keep a list of residents who need help to evacuate.
  6. Buyers and lenders: check WRA riparian maps and past flood records before buying or financing property near the Athi, Stony Athi or their tributaries. Evidence: Machakos County advice, 2026.
  7. County and national government, after the season: commission a flood hazard map and a sediment survey of the Athi through Mavoko, and write river-corridor protection into the next IDeP review. Evidence: Open Questions 4 to 6.

The wider question

Athi River is one of the fastest-growing towns in the Nairobi Metropolitan Region. Its drains, roads and building approvals were designed for the rainfall of the past. The 2024 flood, and the floods of 2026, show that climate has already moved on. The real test this season is not whether it rains heavily. It is whether a town built for yesterday's climate can protect the people living on its lowest ground.

Section 12

12Method, limitations and references

Method

We decomposed the headline question into sub-questions covering hazard, exposure, vulnerability, protection and preparedness; searched government, intergovernmental, academic and news sources; rated each claim; reported conflicting figures side by side; and stress-tested each conclusion before writing. Elevations were sampled from the SRTM 30 m model through the OpenTopoData API, and estate locations were geocoded from OpenStreetMap on 16 September 2026. The research workflow drew on the open-source academic-research-skills framework (source verification, evidence grading, devil's advocate review).

Evidence ratings

★★★★★ government data with independent corroboration · ★★★★ international or national agency · ★★★ peer-reviewed research, or official statements reported by reputable media · ★★ established news media · ★ uncorroborated reports.

Limitations

  • No field visits were made for this review. On-the-ground conditions may differ from what public records show.
  • No river-flow or rainfall-station data for 23 to 24 April 2024 were available to us.
  • Absence of online evidence of works is not proof that works do not exist.
  • Seasonal forecasts are probabilistic and regional. They cannot say whether a specific place will flood.
  • Some 2024 casualty and displacement figures come from early reports and were revised later.
